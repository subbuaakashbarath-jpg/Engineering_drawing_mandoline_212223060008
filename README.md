# 1. Introduction

A wooden mandoline is a simple hand-operated kitchen tool used to cut vegetables and fruits such as potato, raw banana, cucumber, onion and radish into thin, uniform slices. Unlike a knife, the food is pushed across a flat wooden bed toward a fixed steel blade, so every slice has the same thickness. This guide gives complete instructions for building a traditional wooden mandoline (chips slicer) using basic tools and readily available materials. The design is durable, easy to clean and can be customized for different slice thicknesses.

# Key Features
Simple two-plate design with a fixed steel blade set in the gap between the plates
Side rails that keep the plates aligned and give a stable, non-slip frame
Wing-nut adjustment to lock the plate height and control slice thickness
Long wooden handle for a firm, comfortable grip while slicing
Separate holder block that keeps fingers away from the blade
Estimated construction time: 5-7 hours
Estimated cost: Rs. 400-700 (low budget project)

# 5. Step-by-Step Construction Procedure
Step 1: Material Preparation

Mark and cut all wood pieces to the dimensions listed. Use a sharp saw for clean, straight cuts. Number each piece for easy assembly. Inspect all pieces for cracks or knots, especially in the plate that will carry the blade.

Step 2: Cutting the Plates and Handle

Cut the rear plate with a narrower extension at one end to form the handle. Round the handle corners and taper the neck slightly for comfort. Cut the front plate to the same width so both plates fit between the rails.

Step 3: Surface Sanding

Sand all surfaces with 60-grit sandpaper to remove roughness and splinters. Progress through 120-grit and then 220-grit for a smooth finish. Sand along the grain and wipe off all dust with a damp cloth.

Step 4: Blade Seating and Fixing

Mark the blade position on the gap edge between the two plates. File a slight bevel (about 15 to 20 degrees) on the blade edge so it cuts cleanly. Drill small holes along the back of the blade and fix it with rivets or small nails. Check that the edge sits slightly above the front plate surface.

Step 5: Assembly of Frame

Apply adhesive and fix the two side rails parallel to each other. Check square alignment with a carpenter's square. Clamp and let the adhesive dry for 24 hours. The plates should slide freely but without side play.

Step 6: Adjustment Mechanism

Drill a hole through one side rail at the marked position and pass the wing bolt through it. Fit the wing nut so that tightening it locks the front plate at the required height. Test different settings for thin and thick slices.

Step 7: Making the Holder Block

Cut a small block and shape it with a stepped, grooved profile so it grips the vegetable. Add a small lip that acts as a hand guard. Round all edges and sand smooth.

Step 8: Final Finishing

Do not use paint or polyurethane varnish because the tool touches food. Rub food-safe oil (coconut, groundnut or mineral oil) into the wood in 2-3 coats and wipe off the excess. Let it dry for 24 hours before first use.

# 6. Important Tips & Maintenance
Safety Precautions
Always use the holder block; never push the vegetable with bare fingers near the blade
Wear safety goggles when cutting and sanding, and work gloves when handling the blade
Keep the blade covered or wrapped when the slicer is not in use
Place the mandoline on a stable, non-slip surface before slicing
Keep out of reach of children
Quality Assurance Checklist
Aspect	Check Points
Joints	All rail and plate joints are flush and secure
Blade	Sharp, straight, firmly riveted and free of nicks
Surface	No splinters, gaps or rough edges on the slicing bed
Adjustment	Wing nut locks the plate firmly at every setting
Slices	Uniform thickness across the full blade width
Stability	Slicer is level and does not wobble while in use
Durability	No cracks or warping detected
Maintenance Guide

Regular Care

Wipe the blade and plates with a damp cloth immediately after each use
Dry completely; do not soak the wood in water or use a dishwasher
Keep away from excessive moisture and direct sunlight
Store flat or hanging by the handle in a dry place

Annual Maintenance

Inspect the wood for cracks, wear or loose rails
Sharpen or replace the blade if slices become uneven
Re-oil the wood when the surface looks dry
Tighten the wing bolt, rivets and any loose fasteners
# 7. Design Variations & Customization
Possible Modifications
Size Variations: A compact version (10 inches long) suits small kitchens, while a larger version (18 inches) handles big vegetables such as raw banana and yam more easily.
Material Options: Use hardwoods like teak or rosewood for a premium, longer-lasting tool, or good-quality plywood as a budget alternative.
Blade Design: Add a second blade with a serrated or V-shaped edge for wavy chips and julienne-style cuts.
Adjustment: Use two wing bolts, one on each rail, for more precise and even thickness control.
Decorative Enhancements: Carve patterns on the handle, add a hanging hole, or engrave a name or logo.
Functional Add-ons: Add a catch tray below the blade to collect slices, or rubber feet for extra grip.
# 8. Conclusion

Building a wooden mandoline is a rewarding DIY project that combines practical functionality with simple mechanical design. This guide provides the information needed to create a reliable, food-safe slicer that makes uniform slices quickly and saves kitchen time. The process teaches useful woodworking skills, including accurate cutting, blade fixing and building an adjustment mechanism, while producing a useful item that can be customized to personal needs. Whether this is your first woodworking project or you have prior experience, following these steps carefully will result in a durable mandoline that you will be proud to use. Remember that patience, precision and attention to safety are key to achieving excellent results. Happy building!
